PARTIAL · reviewed single-scene result

Ego → simulation → fixed third-person video

Left: unchanged first-person tabletop action. Center: reconstructed SIM v35 with a stable human rig. Right: DiT v8 from the fixed third-person camera, event-aligned to SIM. The 12-second sequence moves the alarm clock, small blue cup/cylinder, and tall cylinder into place.

Pipeline: hand/object perception and metric scene reconstruction → fixed-length shared-torso/contact stabilization → target-camera SIM → full-frame pre-review → H3 generation → full-frame post-review, bounded regeneration or timing repair → EGO | SIM | DiT delivery. The batch implementation includes pinned Sol-Attn and FirstBlockCache, output-error gates, an in-process resident worker, and same-NUMA four-GPU selection. Multi-node RDMA and a cross-CLI persistent service are not implemented or benchmarked. This is visual single-scene evidence, not force-verified physics, arbitrary-RGB automation, or real-robot execution.

Claim boundary: “Completion” means generating an unoccluded, task-equivalent, temporally continuous robot video from the visible context—not uniquely reconstructing the hidden source pixels or proving 3D cloth state, contact force, collision safety, or real-robot execution.

Physical-boundary follow-up · PARTIAL: We added nine fail-closed physical gates, proved monocular scale ambiguity, measured a 9.09× visually compatible force range, and rejected eight evidence-spoof attacks. PhiCheck verdict correct · human D

C02_B_s01 · place the rigid object into the plate

The human graded this D. PhiCheck asked the four task-card checkpoints twice and only 2 and 1 were satisfied, so it returned fail.

  • Human D
  • PhiCheck · correct

HarnessEval-W (paper) gave 0.9945 — essentially its ceiling. All nine of its component axes are near-perfect here: transition correctness 1.000, negative-constraint adherence 1.000, observation quality 0.989, world persistence 0.960. Not one of them asks whether the object actually ended up in the plate. Across the whole set, the 14 cases it correctly passed and the 19 cases it wrongly passed are indistinguishable on all nine axes — the largest gap is 0.019.
